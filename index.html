02 / Selected Work

Brand Identity

Visual identities shaped by research, creative exploration, and intentional decisions about what each brand needs to communicate.

Brand Identity · Packaging Design · Digital Applications

Marmittos

Marmittos is a healthy meal subscription concept created for people who want to eat well without spending time shopping for ingredients or cooking. The visual challenge was to communicate elegance, quality, and convenience while making it clear that the convenience comes from real food rather than an industrialized fast-food experience. I developed a nature-inspired identity grounded in earthy greens, restrained neutrals, organic details, and typography that evokes traditional food preparation. Hand-drawn logo exploration and responsive variations supported a recognizable visual system across packaging and digital content. The project also considers how consistent brand touchpoints and a clear promise of time saved could support customer experience and brand recall.

Brand Identity · Custom Illustration · Digital Applications

Little Luna Pet Store

Little Luna is a pet product e-commerce concept built around the founder’s affection for dogs. The challenge was to look immediately recognizable as a pet brand while creating a distinctive identity that felt personal rather than generic. After briefing and competitor research, I developed a playful visual language using vibrant color, expressive typography, hand-drawn logo exploration, and a custom mascot. Organic curves and flowing lines were chosen to communicate the movement, energy, and softness associated with dogs. The identity extends into packaging and social media, with each application reinforcing the same warm, creative, human-centered personality.

Rebranding · Fashion Brand Identity · Art Direction

Lulima

Lulima is a women’s fashion retailer whose rebranding aimed to support a move toward a higher-income audience, a more elevated product offer, and stronger perceived value. The challenge was to create a feminine identity that felt adult, delicate, confident, and original rather than simply decorative. Through market and competitor research, moodboards, sketches, and typographic exploration, I developed a warmer, more sophisticated palette influenced by luxury fashion references and the founder’s personality. Deliberate changes in letter scale and wave-like forms reference the curves of the female body, adding movement and individuality without losing elegance. The identity was translated into packaging and branded applications as a foundation for the retailer’s new positioning.

Social Media Design · Art Direction · Luxury Brand Communication

Previato Fine Jewelry

During a rebranding initiative for a US-based jewelry brand, I developed the visual direction for its digital communication. The objective was to express product quality, elegance, and mature femininity without relying on crowded compositions or promotional-looking branding. I intentionally photographed or presented the jewelry against softly folded silk: the material, sheen, and wave-like movement were selected to evoke delicacy and refinement while drawing attention to the pieces. Copy was kept sparse to create a quiet, editorial tone, and the logo appeared selectively or was integrated subtly into the layout. Inspired by the principles of quiet luxury, the approach uses visual restraint to make the products—not repeated brand marks—the center of attention.

Editorial Design · Social Media Design · Visual Consistency

Facta

Facta is an Italian digital publication that identifies and debunks misinformation. Although its social media content already used the brand’s black-and-yellow palette, it lacked recurring graphic elements and a consistent editorial language. Rather than proposing an unnecessary full rebrand, I worked from the existing identity: I retained the established colors, introduced a circular graphic motif inspired by the dot within the F of the logo, and selected typography that complemented the original mark. The resulting content system was designed to bring visual continuity to posts on complex topics while making information hierarchy and readability central to the communication.

View Case Study – Facta →

Social Media Design · Personal Brand Communication · Visual Storytelling

Ana Magalhães

Ana Magalhães is an immigration legal consultant whose Instagram communication needed to feel credible without losing the warmth of a personal profile. I worked with her existing colors while making them visually compatible with the burgundy used by her partner company, Kravitz & Guerra. Serif typography brought an editorial, professional tone; real photographs, particularly those connected to Ana’s own experience of living in the United States, helped humanize the content. The strategic narrative went beyond explaining immigration services: the profile was designed to speak to the aspiration behind those services—the possibility of building a life in America. This balance of professional trust and personal storytelling guided the visual decisions throughout the project.
